# Phase 80 — the rescue skill keeps the knowledge and the mechanics, and the repair is the agent's

[names-and-reasons-not-laws](../../../docs/names-and-reasons-not-laws.md) governs. Its § "The harness as a sensor, not a regulator" says: "A law written ahead does the opposite: it decides before the case is known, and it breaks what it governs when the case arrives. Where rules were added, the quality of the work fell; where they were removed, it rose." Its § "Before a sentence goes into a durable text" asks of each sentence: "is it a name, a reason or a question — or an answer written for the reader in advance?" The doc names a rescue report among the parts that work, "a story told round by round, in plain prose", and says nothing of a rescue's classification, categories or menu; its principle is what governs them.

The account behind the phase: before `task-rescue` existed, the user asked an agent to read the orchestrator's artifacts, and that was enough for it to understand the task's problem and repair it. The skill came because explaining what the artifacts are and how the orchestrator works grew tiresome. It then grew ways to rescue no one ordered. The real ways are few and are mechanics: delete the artifacts, or roll back to a given state. Every decision about the logical repair is the agent's own, as is the code's quality, and fences degrade both.

Where `src/skills/task-rescue/SKILL.md` stands against that:
- **What stays.** Knowledge the agent lacks, through `orchestrator-artifacts`: what the artifacts are and where they lie, the signals, the sidecar fields; as facts, stated once, that an escalated stop is recorded in the sidecar (`step: escalated` and the `escalation` record) and that a resumed implementer session can come back having produced no diff; § "Step 1 — Discover artifacts" with its capture of the report facts; and the experience that Step 2's classification and Step 3's categories compress, how the orchestrator actually fails, which the agent can get nowhere else. Mechanics: the deletions, the sidecar `step` values the orchestrator accepts and § "Valid sidecar `step` states", and § "Step 5.7 — Write the durable report", which the user reads for analysis.
- **What goes: the answers.** Step 2 routes each class to a depth ("repair depth starts at spec"). Step 3 fixes the form of the root, a report that must end on "a standalone root-cause sentence … stating the missing or wrong constraint in the spec/contract". Step 4 hands out a menu of depths with ready-made texts, the escalation and non-convergence options with a recommendation, and the scope-overload and stale-implementer flags. Step 5.5 offers each matching open task "+ <proposed clause>". The classes and categories are experience; written as answers each routed to a depth, they became fences, and a root that is not a missing constraint has no place to be said.
- **Not placed by the cut yet.** Step 3's chronological narrative, Step 5.6, "Pin disposed observations", § "What NOT to do", which holds mechanics (committed files, other slugs) and fences on the repair's logic together, and the description and opening paragraphs, which describe a repair keyed to a depth the user picks.

**What replaces the answers: questions a rescue asks a stopped task.** The same experience, directing the agent's look and deciding nothing; the answer and the repair are the agent's, found with the user. A question stays only with a case behind it, and it asks of an observable, never a reading of one.
- Does the same finding recur, or is it a new one each round while severity falls? — Step 3, "Recurring issues"; cases `0025`, `0026`, `0031`, `0032`, `0035`, the field paragraph below and the shape signal in [a contract-task pays only for the unit that holds the hazard](0250-a-contract-task-pays-only-for-the-unit-that-holds-the-hazard.md).
- Did the spec leave the planner something to decide or to enumerate? — Step 3, "Specification gap"; cases `0024`, `0025`, `0026`, `0031`, `0035`, `0037`.
- Is anything the spec says false of the code, or did its own text — a check, a fence — cause the findings? — cases `0027`, `0031`, `0033`, `0034`.
- Should the task exist as written, and is the system ready for it? — Step 4, "Scope-overload flag"; cases `0024`, `0036`, `0038`.

What the field showed, in another repository: a stall at plan review in which every round said nothing was blocking, the risk fell, and each round found a new class of precision nit, was the signal that the surface the plan had to pin was too large to settle in a few rounds. It was seen over tangled code and over one unit holding two behaviours; the repair was a split, and comparable tasks with one behaviour each had passed in one round. No preset reading of that stall said so; as a question it is one the agent can ask.

**Cases.** Rescues collect here as the evidence that the agent finds the repair's form itself: it deleted, split, corrected what was false, added by enumeration, and went above the task. The reports live in `.ai-factory/rescue-reports/tradeoxy_core/`; each case gives the root in a line and the form the repair took.
- **`0024-77-1-a-seam-whose-write-was-left-to-be-invented.md`.** The plans failed, round after round, on a release-and-abort mechanism the spec left to be invented; the user then rejected the mechanism the task was moving, not its seam, a live pass skipping a run's orders through an exemption list. Form: nothing added to the spec. A new phase ahead of the task, this one declared invalid until it lands, the attempt reset in full, the contract line and the spec left for a re-cut.
- **`0025-79-2-two-censuses-left-to-the-planner.md`.** Censuses left to the planner over tangled code: the comments a new policy falsifies, the tests its branches owe; and a rule in the spec that was false. Form: an addition by enumeration, the comments listed by quoted text and the tests named, with one rule replacing the false exception. The next task's spec gained facts the reviews had found for it.
- **`0026-79-3-2-a-third-census-the-spec-swept-by-symbol.md`.** Another census: the spec swept the retiring mechanism by its symbols and not by the prose that narrates it. Form: an addition, the stale comments listed by fragment and a rule about such prose, and the same rule written into later specs, so a rule travelled and not only facts.
- **`0027-79-3-2-a-check-written-into-the-spec-by-its-own-rescue.md`.** The previous rescue's own addition caused the failure: an acceptance sweep written into the spec, over a pattern that also matched code, which no implementation could pass. Form: a removal. The acceptance sentence deleted outright, the sweep kept only as the list's provenance with a narrower pattern, the plan's task for it deleted, and the owed case restated to name the read it guards. The report does not say whether the copies of that rule in the later specs were revisited.
- **`0031-77-6-1-a-fake-of-what-already-existed.md`.** The spec asked for a fake of a collaborator that already existed as a small synchronous class, named a stop point that is no await, and left the suite's mutability and the source of an outcome's message unsaid. Form: a removal and additions. The suite runs over the real record; the stop points an abort can land on, the abort reasons and the source of the message are named; the next task may correct the suite and never weaken it. Neighbouring specs were repaired with it.
- **`0032-77-6-1-one-unit-two-behaviours.md`.** The same task after its repair: one unit holding two behaviours. Form: a split, the field report above.
- **`0033-77-6-3-unchanged-cases-that-had-to-change.md`.** The spec's own text was false: cases called unchanged that had to drop a mock, and a precedent wrong for the shape of the outcome. Form: the statements corrected, and the door's suite required to pin every outcome; a neighbouring spec gained the clause that fits.
- **`0034-89-2-fixtures-that-opened-off-the-grid.md`.** The plan said no existing assertion touched the stamp it changed, and the spec carried a false claim about a cursor; the recorded fixtures opened off the minute grid, so replaying them through the changed close failed several cases byte for byte. The governing doc was already right, a bar opens and closes on the grid; the fixtures described bars that cannot exist. The implementer did not rewrite them: it stopped and laid out ways forward, and the user chose one. Form: the spec corrected and its breakage named, the generator moved onto a minute boundary with every scenario to be regenerated, a full reset, the implementer's code reverted to HEAD; no doc is among what the repair changed. This case went right: the agent brought the question instead of inventing an answer, and one question to the user settled it.
- **`0035-89-3-stale-words-found-one-round-at-a-time.md`.** The task moved a detector onto the grid and retired a mechanism, and its spec did not list the places in the touched files where the retired mechanism is named in words: test titles, comments, banners. The first plan review found three real errors, which the plan fixed: a label with no grid assumed impossible, a wrong figure in one case, a new interface named without its marker. The second review's cosmetic point led the plan to reword one case's comment and say to reword nothing else, while the same file held more titles and comments narrating the retired floor, which the plan then added. The third review found the section banners still naming the deleted function. Each round surfaced a new batch of words about the old mechanism, because the spec never listed them. The governing spec was not at issue. Form: an addition by enumeration, at spec and plan depth. The spec and the contract line carry the rule that a task retiring a mechanism lists every place in the touched files where it is named in words, with the search that finds them and the census of what the search reaches on HEAD, quoted; the plan names the titles, comments and banners it rewords, and its postcondition is that the detector's spec file matches neither pattern. The deferred observation all three reviews carried was routed, at the user's choice, into a new task placed before this one. The reviews were deleted, the sidecar set to `planned:1`, and the plan kept.
- **`0036-90-1-a-drop-migration-the-generator-cannot-write.md`.** The spec asked for a generated migration that drops the table of a removed entity. The repository's rules forbid a hand-written migration and the generator cannot emit a drop for a table with no entity, so the plan's own stop, if the generator emitted no drop, would fire on every run. The plan reviewer escalated: the decision was outside its authority. It offered ways forward: generate inverted against a scratch database and swap up and down, record a rules exception for table drops, generate from an empty database and extract, or leave the table in place. None of them removed anything. The governing doc was not at issue; it says nothing of migrations. Form: a removal, by the user's ruling. The table holds no data, so the migration that creates it is deleted, no drop migration is written, and the rules get nothing, so that a case like this escalates again and does not pass under a standing exception. The application's database keeps its empty table and the migration's row, and nothing drops them. The decision is recorded in the task's spec and contract line; the plan, the plan review and the sidecar were deleted; no other open spec of the phase touches a migration. The report stands as the record of the attempt; its author corrected the cause in a message of 2026-10-15, which is in no file here. The report named the generator's limit, and the root was a ruling of the user's that never reached any memory: the databases hold no data, so migrations are rewritten and not accumulated. The spec was written without it and asked for a new drop migration on top of the creating one, adding where the standing practice removes; the generator's limit only made the gap loud. The user, on 2026-09-18: «все базы — мёртвые, их надо дропать и накатывать свежие миграции… про данных нет». His words on the cause: «скорей тут ошибка — я говорил, что данных нет и мы можем миграции переделывать а не наращивать. Это видимо потерялось.» The author has since written the ruling into its buffer.
- **`0037-90-6-1-a-seed-that-belonged-to-no-one.md`.** The task stopped in planning, with no code written and nothing escalated; the governing doc was not violated by any plan. The first review asked for the seed order to be a red case and not a comment, and found cases that could not tell a fresh seed from a kept close. The second plan fixed those and, by its size, brought inconsistencies of its own: a constant declared in one task and used in another, a shutdown variant that asserted nothing, an interface block that promised what it did not give. The third review reached the layer the spec never decided: no case made the stored-close read fail, so an unguarded implementation would pass and leave the seed rejected with nothing reporting why; and no case covered a resubscribe landing while the earlier seed was pending, so two wrong implementations passed every scenario. One spec said the seed is never in flight twice for a pair, the other that a resubscribe starts fresh, and neither said whose the seed is. The report classifies it as a specification gap in which the seed path drew findings in the first and third rounds. Form: an addition, at spec and plan depth. Two rulings were written into both specs: a seed belongs to the subscription that started it, so a resubscribe starts its own seed and the earlier result is dropped whenever it lands; and a failing read of the stored close counts as nothing stored. The contract line names the failing reads and the resubscribe case among its red specs; the plan states both rulings and gained the cases for them; three review observations that belong to the implementation task were folded into its spec; the reviews were deleted and the sidecar set to `planned:1` with the planner, elapsed and reviewer ids kept. On the depth, by the report's author in a message of 2026-10-15, which is in no file here: the author's hand proposed a full reset, on the ground that the decisions the spec leaves open are the spec's job. The author held spec and plan instead, because those decisions are how-level and all three plan reviewers had accepted them.

- **`0038-90-7-2-a-bar-that-waited-for-the-exchange.md`.** The task made the exchange's closed minute the `1m` bar and folded every wider bar from those minutes, so a bar's close waited on a kline the exchange does not promise on time — about a minute late. Three plan reviews each repaired the forming-bar contract at a bucket boundary with an invented rule, and each rule broke a different reader. The model was the phase's and its governing doc's, not the task's: the user ruled that a bar closes the instant its time comes, from core's own trades, and the exchange's minute only checks it; the 500 ms grace on a second goes too. Form: a removal at the phase. Phase 90 closed with what had landed, this task and the next deleted with their specs, the minute assembler already landed to be deleted, the docs rewritten to the model, and a header-only phase carrying the work. The removal was the user's; none of the reviews proposed it. Where the wrong step entered is recorded in the author's snapshot and was given by core 130's message of 2026-10-15: the user's quote on Phase 90's root stands there, and the step "the exchange's 1m kline stream gives the minute" follows it with no ruling of his; the grace had none either. See phase 81's note, [a task carries its source](0227-a-task-carries-its-source-and-a-find-without-one-goes-to-the-user.md).
