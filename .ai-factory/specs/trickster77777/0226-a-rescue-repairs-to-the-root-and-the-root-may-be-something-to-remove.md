# Phase 80 — the rescue skill keeps the knowledge and the mechanics, and the repair is the agent's

[names-and-reasons-not-laws](../../../docs/names-and-reasons-not-laws.md) governs. Its § "The harness as a sensor, not a regulator" says: "A law written ahead does the opposite: it decides before the case is known, and it breaks what it governs when the case arrives. Where rules were added, the quality of the work fell; where they were removed, it rose." Its § "Before a sentence goes into a durable text" asks of each sentence: "is it a name, a reason or a question — or an answer written for the reader in advance?" The doc names a rescue report among the parts that work, "a story told round by round, in plain prose", and says nothing of a rescue's classification, categories or menu; its principle is what governs them.

The account behind the phase: before `task-rescue` existed, the user asked an agent to read the orchestrator's artifacts, and that was enough for it to understand the task's problem and repair it. The skill came because explaining what the artifacts are and how the orchestrator works grew tiresome. It then grew ways to rescue no one ordered. The real ways are few and are mechanics: delete the artifacts, or roll back to a given state. Every decision about the logical repair is the agent's own, as is the code's quality, and fences degrade both.

Where `src/skills/task-rescue/SKILL.md` stands against that:
- **What stays.** Knowledge the agent lacks, through `orchestrator-artifacts`: what the artifacts are and where they lie, the signals, the sidecar fields; as facts, stated once, that an escalated stop is recorded in the sidecar (`step: escalated` and the `escalation` record) and that a resumed implementer session can come back having produced no diff; § "Step 1 — Discover artifacts" with its capture of the report facts; and the experience that Step 2's classification and Step 3's categories compress, how the orchestrator actually fails, which the agent can get nowhere else. Mechanics: the deletions, the sidecar `step` values the orchestrator accepts and § "Valid sidecar `step` states", and § "Step 5.7 — Write the durable report", which the user reads for analysis.
- **What goes: the answers.** Step 2 routes each class to a depth ("repair depth starts at spec"). Step 3 fixes the form of the root, a report that must end on "a standalone root-cause sentence … stating the missing or wrong constraint in the spec/contract". Step 4 hands out a menu of depths with ready-made texts, the escalation and non-convergence options with a recommendation, and the scope-overload and stale-implementer flags. Step 5.5 offers each matching open task "+ <proposed clause>". The classes and categories are experience; written as answers each routed to a depth, they became fences, and a root that is not a missing constraint has no place to be said.
- **Not placed by the cut yet.** Step 3's chronological narrative, Step 5.6, "Pin disposed observations", § "What NOT to do", which holds mechanics (committed files, other slugs) and fences on the repair's logic together, and the description and opening paragraphs, which describe a repair keyed to a depth the user picks.
- **How a rescue ends.** The skill puts its questions to the user through `AskUserQuestion` menus: the depth, the escalation options and the non-convergence options at Step 4, and the question about applying a clause to open tasks at Step 5.5. The user wants the opposite: «рескью, тут я хочу читать отчёты и что б агент на паузе ждал… но не как вопрос, этот интерфейс- бред какой то». A rescue writes its report, a chronicle extended while the rescue is hot (the user's ruling of 2026-10-21, «Летопись спасения»), and stops until the user's word in the conversation; the report is the interface, and no question is put to him.
- **Who found what.** The core reports name a finder only in passing sentences, and the finder of the root cause is often not stated at all; what they name most is what stopped the run, a review cycle spent or an escalation. They show a reviewer round or an escalation as what stopped the run, the user as the one who ruled, a head as a second reader and the editor as the one who caught the head's own slips, so what share the user caught and what share the heads caught cannot be told from them. The finder line lives in the family's chronicle of catches, `.ai-factory/chronicle/skills.md`, where every case gathers and a rescue's is linked to its report; the rescue report carries no finder section. The user: «У нас есть летопись кто что поймал и где вектор развернул, пусть там собираются все случаи и рескью тоже. Там может быть ссылка на отчёт… А рескью эта возможность вообще никчему. Его дело таски спасать. И отчёт о спасении.»
- **The rollback runs on the user's word.** A rescue diagnoses, writes the chronicle with the rollback it intends, pauses, and on his word applies the rollback and extends the chronicle. Asked whether the rollback runs before the pause or after his word, the user: «да, я хочу видеть отчёты рескью». How to ask is the agent's own: the skill prescribes no question form and no menu text, and `AskUserQuestion` stays available to the agent.

**What replaces the answers: questions a rescue asks a stopped task.** The same experience, directing the agent's look and deciding nothing; the answer and the repair are the agent's, found with the user. A question stays only with a case behind it, and it asks of an observable, never a reading of one.
- Does the same finding recur, or is it a new one each round while severity falls? — Step 3, "Recurring issues"; cases `0025`, `0026`, `0031`, `0032`, `0035`, `0037`, `0039`, `0040`, `0041`, `0042`, `0044`, the field paragraph below and the shape signal in [a contract-task pays only for the unit that holds the hazard](0250-a-contract-task-pays-only-for-the-unit-that-holds-the-hazard.md). `0037` and `0039` answer it the same way: a new finding each round, with no fall, points to the task's shape, and the repair that follows is a split. In `0039` the report's own repair was the split; in `0037` the report repaired by addition at spec and plan depth, and the split came after, as phase 90's note records, on core 130's message. `0040` and `0041` answer its first half: the same finding recurs, in a different face each round (a fetch bounded by time where the stream and the second store are bounded by order), and the repair that followed was a split. `0042` is the exception, and it is to the repair, not to the signal: the reading that a new finding each round with no fall points at the task's shape, and that the repair is a split, never reaches a split that separates a task's tests from the body they test. The report's own account of `0042` is a stall whose severity fell and stayed low, and it names no scope overload; the exception to the split comes from core 130's message of 2026-10-20, in the case itself.
- Did the spec leave the planner something to decide or to enumerate? — Step 3, "Specification gap"; cases `0024`, `0025`, `0026`, `0031`, `0035`, `0037`, `0044`.
- Is anything the spec says false of the code, or did its own text — a check, a fence — cause the findings? — cases `0027`, `0031`, `0033`, `0034`, `0040`, `0041`, `0042`, `0044`.
- Should the task exist as written, and is the system ready for it? — Step 4, "Scope-overload flag"; cases `0024`, `0036`, `0038`, `0044`.

What the field showed, in another repository: a stall at plan review in which every round said nothing was blocking, the risk fell, and each round found a new class of precision nit, was the signal that the surface the plan had to pin was too large to settle in a few rounds. It was seen over tangled code and over one unit holding two behaviours; the repair was a split, and comparable tasks with one behaviour each had passed in one round. No preset reading of that stall said so; as a question it is one the agent can ask.
