# Phase 42 — the number resolves, and resolves wrongly

The global CLAUDE.md § "Grounding claims" and `docs/reference-by-name.md` § "Granularity, not size" both list a numbered item among the addresses that survive insertion: the global's "A heading, a bolded rule, a symbol, a numbered item survives every insertion above it," and the doc's "the unit of naming is not the section. It is anything that will be depended on — a heading, a bold lead-in, a numbered item, a named clause" — the second stating this in the very sentence that rules "the section" out. The two are compatible only if a numbered item and a section's own number are different things, and neither text says what makes them different. That silence is the width the mis-citation went through.

The distinction neither text states: a once-assigned identifier — a task number, a numbered rule — is appended and never recomputed, so an insertion elsewhere does not touch it; a running ordinal is recomputed from the document's current structure, so an insertion above it moves what it points at. Only the first kind survives what the rule says a numbered item survives, and nothing in either sentence marks which kind a given number is.

The second property, absent from both texts in any form, is why the error stayed invisible: an address either carries content that can disagree with its target, or it carries none. A title is redundant with the section it names, so a wrong title disagrees with what it lands on and the mismatch shows on arrival. A number is orthogonal to its target, so a wrong number lands on a real section that reads plausibly regardless. This form is therefore neither of the two the governing spec already knows: not rot, because nothing was renumbered; not silence, because it looks exactly like an address — it invites the question of whether it resolves, and answers yes, to the wrong thing. The only check that reaches it is walking to the target fresh at the moment of writing, which is how it was in fact caught.

§ "The check"'s own stated discriminator — "ask whether the reference would still resolve if the sentence beside it were replaced" — passes this form clean: a section number does not resolve by proximity to anything beside it, so the test certifies it as a name. The test is not wrong; it tests one failure mode, and this is a different one. This is a defect in the governing spec itself, not only in its readers — it leads its code, and it is in scope for this phase.

The live carriers, with the nuance intact: seven bare citations of `orchestrator-artifacts` § 1, § 3 and § 6, across `roadmap-prune` and `task-rescue`, into a target whose own headings already carry the name beside the number — `## 6. Status-marker grammar` and its siblings. Because those numbers are baked into the heading text they do not shift on their own; nothing in that file promises they never move, and an ordinary renumbering breaks all seven at once. The name is available at no cost today, and none of the seven takes it.

The governing spec carries its own seam on this. § "The check" says a position address never enters "a spec, a roadmap line, or a document," and its next paragraph exempts "a task spec, a plan, a work-order" by name — a phase note appears in neither list, while `roadmap-outline-deep` § "Step 1 — Write the phase note" prescribes "a `file:line` where a claim needs one" into exactly that artifact. Whether that instruction is legitimate turns on a question the doc has not answered.

One rule states itself three times with three non-identical member lists: the global's "a heading, a bolded rule, a symbol, a numbered item," `docs/reference-by-name.md`'s "a heading, a bold lead-in, a numbered item, a named clause," and `docs/paired-loop.md` § "What crosses the channel"'s "a heading, a bolded rule, a symbol, a unique string." No two lists agree on their members. Named here, not resolved.

`docs/sakshi-harness/skill-cycle.md` states the opposite repair from the one `command-pin-gaps` performs on the identical act: its "value-дыры пинятся точным значением из кода с цитатой `file:line`" against the skill's own "never a line number, because the spec outlives the numbering it was written against." One of the two describes what the other does not do.

The edits this phase turns up to `docs/` — the check's own discriminator, the numbered-item/section distinction, the phase-note seam, the three-enumeration drift, and `docs/sakshi-harness/skill-cycle.md`'s stale description — all stop here, recorded rather than made; this pass writes nothing under `docs/`.
