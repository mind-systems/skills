# Phase 48 — an echo is about whether a second reading exists, not about who delegated

What decides an echo is whether a second, independently produced reading exists. `docs/paired-loop.md` § "Where the split falls" states it: a question of judgment needs two independent readings reconciled, and anything that leaks one reader's conclusion into the other destroys them. The document no longer carries the origin-keyed passage the phase first named, and it carries no round rule at all, so nothing in it diverges.

What still diverges is in `agent-architect`, § "Relay on the marker; author the apply work-order and your own legwork": "A report on your own delegated legwork carries no second opinion … Reading its agreement as corroboration mistakes an echo for evidence". The sentence keys on who asked and holds unconditionally. It is right for the ordinary delegated round, where the head did no work of its own on the question and only one reading exists. It is wrong as the general rule it is phrased as: a head that walks the same ground in parallel and withholds its own read produces two readings, and withholding is only possible where the head did the work.

What the skill leaves as it should be. `editor.md` says nothing of how its report is weighed, and its analysis-mode text is origin-neutral; the weighing is the head's business, so the repair is the skill's alone. The round-closing rule lives in `agent-architect` § "Nothing closes a round before the report on it exists"; the document states none, and the earlier absence the phase noted in the document is settled by the compression that removed the passage around it.
