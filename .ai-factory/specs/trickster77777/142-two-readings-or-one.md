# Phase 48 — whether a second reading exists at all

Put to one case — the head delegates a question of judgment and withholds its own read — the two passages answer differently. § "The user's marker" keys on origin and answers unconditionally: "When the head delegates its own work, there is no second opinion in the result — it is a hand, and treating its agreement as corroboration mistakes an echo for evidence." § "Where the split falls" keys on the question's type and names leakage as what destroys independence: "A question of judgment... needs two independent readings reconciled. There the independence is the whole point, and anything that leaks one reader's conclusion into the other destroys it."

What a reconcile actually needs is both halves at once: two readings, produced independently. § "Where the split falls" states exactly that — "two independent readings reconciled" — existence and independence together, in one phrase. § "The user's marker" collapses the pair to a single question: who asked.

Origin looks right because, in the ordinary delegated round, the head has done no separate work on the question at all — only one reading exists, and there is genuinely nothing to reconcile against. Passage 1 is correct about that case. It generalizes past it: a delegated round in which the head walked the same ground in parallel and withheld its own read produces two readings, and withholding is only possible where the head did the work in the first place. The finding: the sentence is right about the case it was written for, and wrong as the general rule it is phrased as.

The structural point that settles which variable is binding: the architect's own current read of an open question, a diagnosis still forming, reaches the hand in no round at all — it lives in the conversation, never in a channel-message — so contamination is prevented by the channel's own design, not by this rule. A blanket rule keyed on origin would be strange to need if contamination were the only thing at stake; what it is actually protecting against is counting one reading twice.

`agent-architect` carries the spec's own sentence almost word for word: the spec's "there is no second opinion in the result... treating its agreement as corroboration mistakes an echo for evidence" against the skill's "carries no second opinion... Reading its agreement as corroboration mistakes an echo for evidence." The skill is not independently wrong; it inherits the imprecision by construction, which is why the repair starts at the document.

`editor.md` says nothing anywhere about how its own report is weighed, and its Analysis-mode text is origin-neutral. This is the right shape rather than a gap: the weighing is the head's business, and a hand told how its answer will be counted is a hand with the head's expectation in hand before it has reasoned at all. The repair is therefore one-sided — the document and the skill that carries its sentence, not the hand.

Kept separate, as two plain absences rather than disagreements. The governing spec never states the round-closing rule in any wording — "round" appears in it once, in § "What crosses the channel", as the bare definition "a unit of work sent out and reported back on" — while `agent-architect` holds that rule as a section of its own. And nothing in the spec, `agent-architect`, `editor.md` or `architect-editor-engine` describes positively what the head may say to the user while a round is open, only what it may not. The holding message the field report proposes would attach to a rule the spec does not carry.
