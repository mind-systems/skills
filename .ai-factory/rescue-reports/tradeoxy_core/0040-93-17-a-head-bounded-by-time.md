# 93.17 — A head bounded by time

**Project:** tradeoxy_core
**Date:** 2026-10-16
**Stopped at:** planned:3
**Elapsed before the rescue:** 1262
**Sessions:** planner: 6516cbb3-6856-4c24-bd03-96d1f1fde358; plan-reviewer-1: 5b81caab-cf9c-467a-b677-b4e3dec3f81f; plan-reviewer-2: 59cf1822-8e20-4cd0-b436-abb11db20d47; plan-reviewer-3: c8d72b64-8d26-4cf8-8f1e-96d541ac4277

The task stopped in planning; no code was written and nothing escalated. The governing doc, what a bar is, was violated by the task spec, not by any plan. Its catch-up paragraph bounds a connect's head by id: the head of the current minute comes from the exchange's aggregate trades «up to the first id the live stream delivered». The spec bounds the head by time, up to the start of the live seconds, and the plans followed the spec. The phase note records the neighbouring question as open and left to decomposition: how a caught-up second enters the second store. The failure is the task spec failing to carry what the doc and the note already state.

The task fills the throwing `PairCatchUp` shell. On a connect it reads the closed minutes and the head of the current minute from the exchange's trades; on a reconnect it reads the skipped window by id; on an id gap while connected it marks the forming bars, fetches the lost trades and amends them; and it provides the unit and wires it into the watch loop. The watch loop calls the clock's connect hook only after the first batch of trades has returned, so the live seconds begin at the next whole second, later than every trade of that batch, and the second store silently discards every trade below that start. The head, fetched by time, is the only way those trades reach the bars.

The first review found that the plan's rule «a first connect treats a gap in its first batch as a gap while connected» counted that gap's trades twice: they lie in the head, and the amend folds them again with no cover check, so the bars of a minute or more would go out with doubled volume and no warning. It raised three further edges, a reconnect run with nothing to fetch that would run synchronously, an unpinned ordering inside the connect hook, and watch-loop wiring that could fail without a trace. It also parked two observations: a reconnect loses its own first-batch trades, since it reads only the gap's ids and the store discards them, and a reconnect gap's trades outside the skipped window are dropped without a mark.

The planner answered the double count with a filter that drops, from a gap's trades, those older than the end of the head, and fixed the other three. The second review found that a connect in the last second before a minute boundary asks for a closed minute that is still forming, which breaks the precondition the minutes reader leaves to its caller: the exchange fill skips the unfinished minute, the read throws, and the whole connect fails, about once in sixty. The plan added a timer-based wait. The review also parked that the newly landed rule, that a primed bucket is not amended, turns the suite case «completes a gap and a pending connect run independently» red in both orders, and that the new spec's radius does not name the suite.

The third review found the wait is a single timer that can fire early against the wall clock and still throw, and a page-size comment that repeats the constant meant to be its only home. Both are Low. It restated, harder, the observation parked from the first: the head is fetched before its last second has ended, so trades matched between the exchange read and the start of the live seconds are in neither the head nor the stream, and the second store discards them; the bar then goes out whole and short of volume on nearly every liquid connect. The plan's own filter drops a later gap's trades in that same window. The reviewer judged the fix to be a contract decision, since the red suite pins an immediate head fetch bounded by time.

Each fix held and each review found the opposite face of one boundary: the head and the reconnect fetch end at a time, where the stream and the second store begin at a time and an id. The double count, the lost first batch, the lost last second, the minute-boundary failure and the filter's own loss are one defect, and the guard added for the fifth, a timer, is the sign that the mechanism was the thing to question.

Classification: specification gap, with a scope-overload signal; the same boundary recurred in every review.

> The spec must end every catch-up at the trade before the first one the live seconds take, by id, so that the stream and the catch-up never overlap and never leave a gap; had it bounded the head and the reconnect fetch that way, no trade would have fallen between the fetches and the stream, and neither the filter nor the wait would have been needed.

## What was done

The repair is at spec depth, to the doc's bound. One rule serves every run: a catch-up ends at the trade before the first one the live seconds take, and the live stream is ordered by id, so the fetch is every trade older than the seconds' start. The head is bounded by that trade's id, known as soon as the stream delivers a trade the seconds accept. Closed minutes run to the start of the minute holding the last second before the seconds' start, a minute that is always closed, so the minutes reader's precondition holds with no timer. A reconnect fetches by id from the last seen id to the trade before the first taken one, which covers the skipped window and the first batch's discarded trades. A silent stream makes the head end at what the exchange holds, the ledger take its last id, and the next live trade show any gap through the ordinary gap path. The filter and the wait are not written; a gap inside a connect's range starts no run, and one above the bound runs as before.

The doc wording in the pipeline doc and in what a bar is now states the bound; the reconnect sentence of both changed with it. The task was split by the roadmap's sub-numbering rule. The first part, 93.17.1, fills `PairCatchUp` under the rule and has a new spec; it names the red-suite cases that pinned an immediate, time-bounded head fetch and how each changes, and adds `IPairCatchUp.onTrades` and `ITradeIdLedger.advance` as the seams the rule needs. The second part, 93.17.2, wires the catch-up into the module and the watch loop and keeps the old spec number, rewritten to the wiring. The registered-series spec now cites the new spec for the runs, and the radius of the spec that stops amending primed buckets names the suite case of a gap beside a pending connect, whose expectation becomes warned bars.

The attempt's plan, its three plan reviews and its sidecar were deleted after this report was written. The deferred observations in those reviews were not pinned: the head's bound and the reconnect loss were routed into the new spec, the suite interplay into the primed-bucket spec's radius, and the architecture-map note was left for the doc owner.
