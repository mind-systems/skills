# 90.7.2 — A bar that waited for the exchange

**Project:** tradeoxy_core
**Date:** 2026-10-15
**Stopped at:** planned:3
**Elapsed before the rescue:** 2122
**Sessions:** planner: 5f9b2f8d-5e80-4129-bfbe-df901006a261; plan-reviewer-1: 5c8e4ccc-59ce-47a4-8851-53edf395067f; plan-reviewer-2: 4d588ee8-a8df-4135-bd15-babc1d50c53a; plan-reviewer-3: 1123fe63-dd57-41fa-ac05-9b508bdaae60

The task stopped in planning; no code was written and nothing escalated. The governing doc, what a bar is, was not violated by any plan: it states no latency for a bar's close and no rule for the forming bar at a bucket boundary, and the nearest clauses, that a time bar closes at the instant the next bar on the grid opens and that only the forming bar may be partial, were not breached.

The task swapped the source of the `1m` bar: the exchange's closed minute replaces a minute folded from core's seconds, and every bar of five minutes and up folds from those minutes. Its spec pinned what such a bar closes on. A bucket closes when a minute of the next bucket arrives, which the spec's five-minute and three-minute cases state outright. It also pinned one forming-bar case, a forming minute inside the same bucket as the closed minutes, and it said nothing about a forming minute that lies in a later bucket than the one still open, about a minute that ended without being delivered, or about a bucket left over from before a pause. The exchange delivers a minute only when the following minute's kline arrives, so the last minute of a bucket is delivered about when the next bucket opens, and the bucket itself closes only when the next bucket's first minute is delivered, about a minute after that. Every bar of a minute or more would therefore close about a minute after its grid end, where today it closes about two seconds after, and the alerts computed on it would reach the broker that much later. No one had examined that consequence, and the spec did not name it.

The first plan followed the spec literally. Its review accepted the core logic: the feed cases traced green and the forming-bar arithmetic of the spec's case matched. It blocked on four things. The architecture maps still named the detector as the `1m` emitter. The series resolver would label a 90-second width a minute series, which production never reaches because the timeframe resolver already refuses it. The feed loop's promise never to reject was stated and not built. The list of detector-suite cases to convert was incomplete. It also noted, as non-blocking, that the forming bar loses a minute that has ended but is not yet delivered, and that the spec's claim that ccxt resends its whole kline cache does not hold for the pooled instance, which runs ccxt's default of returning only the entries touched since the last read.

The second plan fixed all four. Where the spec was silent it decided for itself: when the forming minute lies in a later bucket than the open one, the forming bar is the forming minute alone. It also added a set of folded minutes to each minute-folded bucket, so that a minute delivered again after a restart would not be counted twice. The second review found the first decision unsafe. For about a minute after every boundary, a reader receives a bar for the new bucket while the old bucket's close is still undelivered. Live indicator state then takes a phantom entry, because the engine decides replace or append by comparing open times and the confirmed older bar arrives after the newer forming one; the chart is handed an older bar after a newer one and throws. It noted, again as non-blocking, that the window exists only because bars close a minute late. It also found the set of folded minutes untested for the restart case and the test helper's overrides untyped.

The third plan replaced the rule with "the open bucket alone, held back while a minute is pending". The third review found that this rule created new problems. The streaming gateway overwrites the served close with the live price, so for that minute it publishes the old bucket with the new bucket's price as its close; the same overwrite already exists for about two seconds today and would last about sixty. With no adjacency bound, a bucket left over from before a pause is served as the live bar to a chart that has just loaded history up to now. The set of folded minutes grows with the bucket's width, about three hundred thousand entries for a seven-month bar, for every pair and every calendar label. And the re-delivery case pins one debug log where the planned code logs four, one per minute-folded series. None of the four was folded back, and the pattern across the three reviews is plain: each repair of the forming-bar contract invented a rule, and each rule broke a different reader, because the spec never stated the contract at the boundary and the planner was deciding product behaviour alone.

The user then ruled on the model itself, and that was the deeper defect. A bar must close the instant its time comes, from core's own trades; nothing on the close path may wait for the exchange, because the exchange does not promise a kline on time, and an indicator cannot be made to wait even a second. Time bars of every width, `1m` included, fold from core's closed seconds and close at their grid end. The exchange's one-minute kline stream is a control only: it compares each closed exchange minute with core's minute, logs a disagreement with both bars, and logs a closed exchange minute with volume where core saw no trade as a stalled trade stream; it never delivers, stores, repairs or times a bar. The 500 millisecond grace the clock gave a second goes too: a second closes at its end, and a trade that reaches core after it is dropped, which the control's log will show. A bar of a minute or more that core sees only in part, because core connected in the middle of it or the trade stream broke in its middle, is caught up from the exchange as soon as core connects or the stream returns, while the bar still forms; a catch-up that has not landed by the close leaves the bar undelivered as closed, and that is logged. Under this model the forming-bar window, the late close, the set of folded minutes and the exchange-minute assembler's role as a deliverer do not exist.

> The phase made the exchange's minute the source and the clock of every bar of a minute or more, so a bar's close waited on a kline the exchange does not promise to send on time; a bar closes on core's own clock and the exchange only checks it.

Classification: specification gap, with the forming-bar window recurring in the second and third reviews and in the deferred observations of all three; the model that produced it was the phase's, not the task's.

## What was done

No depth repair was applied. The user closed Phase 90 with what had landed: the task and the one that completed bars at their close left the roadmap, with their specs, and a header-only phase carries the work of closing on the clock and checking against the exchange. The candle pipeline doc and the what-a-bar-is doc were rewritten to the new model, and the architecture map's pipeline diagram follows them: every time bar folds from core's seconds and closes at its grid end, the kline watcher is a control that only logs, and a pair's trade and kline subscriptions share one pooled instance. Two stale sentences in the pipeline doc and one in the project description were corrected to match.

The attempt's plan, its three plan reviews and its sidecar were deleted after this report was written. The deferred observations in those reviews were not pinned: the task they concern no longer exists.
