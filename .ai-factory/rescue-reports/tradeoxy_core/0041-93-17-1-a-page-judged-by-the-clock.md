# 93.17.1 — A page judged by the clock

**Project:** tradeoxy_core
**Date:** 2026-10-19
**Stopped at:** planned:3
**Elapsed before the rescue:** 1913
**Sessions:** planner: 438f6c2e-7589-4a7d-be04-9a1c5e658ed9; plan-reviewer-1: 41c41660-c872-4f51-b5c9-eaa0842878e1; plan-reviewer-2: 9ab417f2-3a78-4f0f-81c7-9779c83d3dd3; plan-reviewer-3: 096d5b43-2392-4d91-b5d4-55ee66b00b51

The task stopped in planning; no code was written and nothing escalated.

The task fills the throwing `PairCatchUp` under one rule. A catch-up ends at the trade before the first one the live seconds take, B. A connect and a reconnect read alike: a first page by time, then pages by id up to B - 1. A short page while B is unknown parks the run. The plan followed that rule, and every review found the same flaw in a different corner of it. In each case a page fetched by time was taken as proof that the read had reached B - 1, when a time-bounded fetch proves nothing about an id-bounded range.

The first review found that the plan treated an empty first page as «no trade before B». The first page of `tradesSince` covers at most the hour starting at its start, as the `ITradeCatchUpSource` doc says. A connect starts less than a minute before the live seconds, so for a connect that holds. A reconnect starts at `skipped.fromMs`, and nothing bounds the outage to an hour. After a 90-minute outage with no trades in the first hour, the plan would have primed the whole span flat and judged the bars whole, while missing every trade of the last half hour. The review also found that the page size and the usable-id rule each ended up with a second or third home, because the plan exported `TRADE_PAGE_LIMIT` and `tradeIdOf` while the doc comments and `toAggregateTrade` kept their own copies. It parked the observation that the spec itself calls a short by-time page a failure, although `tradesFrom(lastId + 1)` could finish the span.

The planner added `TRADES_SINCE_SPAN_MS` and an `hourReaches` condition. The second review then found the same defect one level down. The first request leaves from `onConnected`, up to a second before `restart.fromMs`. An empty answer proves only that nothing traded up to the moment the exchange answered. A trade between that moment and the seconds' start has an id below B and a time below the start. The stream delivers it, `SecondBars.fill` discards it silently because it is below the start, and `onTrades` skips it because its time is below `fromMs`. That is the race the spec exists to close, and the bar would go out whole and short of volume with no warning. The planner repeated `tradesSince` once B is known (`sinceAfterB`). The review also found that `read` lacked the `pair` it needed to call the source. It parked two things. The first is a stream gap straddling `restart.fromMs`: with ids 105–107 lost and 105 the true first trade at or after the start, B becomes 108 and the gap «below B» starts nothing, while the read keeps 105–107 but «Seconds of a span» folds only trades before `restart.fromMs`, so those trades reach no bar. The second is a gap that reaches B, which is amended while the read also primes the same ids.

The third review applied the second review's reasoning to a non-empty page. A short page requested before B and landing after B hit the table row «Short non-empty page, `b` known → throw». A network race then decided between a logged failure with partial bars and a completed head. The plan's own sentence said a non-empty page's continuation «is always issued after B is known», which the table contradicted. The review also found an uncovered cell, an empty page by id, and restated the parked observation about the by-time page on a reconnect longer than an hour, now a third time.

Every review traced the plan's fixture numbers and found them right. What failed each time was the read rule, which kept gaining a cell: `hourReaches`, then `sinceAfterB`, then a per-request `askedAfterB`.

On the governing doc, the failure violates the clause that the catch-up runs «up to the trade before the first one the live seconds take». It also violates what a bar is, § «A closed bar is partial only when core could not complete it»: «A closed bar is partial only when core could not complete it, and that is logged». Spec `0371` is not innocent: its read rule, «A short page that ends before the trade preceding a known B is a failure», prescribes the failing outcomes of the second and third reviews. The phase note records none of these; it leaves only «which unit owns the trade-id ledger and the REST catch-up, and how a caught-up second enters `SecondBars`» to decomposition. The task spec has to carry the constraint itself.

The failure is the same kind that stopped 93.17 the first time (report `0040`): a fetch bounded by time where the stream and the second store are bounded by order. The rewritten spec moved the end of the read to an id and left three time-based places: the entry read, the judgement of a short page by the instant it lands, and B itself, chosen by `timestamp`. The second and third reviews' scenario is that report's «lost last second» reappearing in the first page.

Classification: specification gap, with a scope-overload signal. The recurring issue is the time-anchored first page. The unit also carried several behaviours (a paged read parked on B, the composition of minutes and seconds, the gap run, run ownership, three shared exports, half a suite rewritten).

> The spec must state that a page ends the read only when it was requested after B was known, with an earlier page serving only as the way in to the ids and continued by `tradesFrom` once B shows, and that a gap touching a trade at or after the seconds' start is never taken for lying inside the range.

## What was done

The repair is at spec depth with a split, on the user's yes to the diagnosis. Four rules replaced the open read:

- The trade fetch starts only once B is known. The run parks, with no timer, until `onTrades` shows B; the closed minutes are still asked at once. From the run's start the read pages by time an hour at a time until a page holds a trade, then by id up to B - 1; an hour with no trade moves on, and reaching the seconds' start with none ends the read empty. A page by id that is not full and ends short of B - 1 is a failure. `hourReaches`, `sinceAfterB`, `askedAfterB`, the repeat and park-after-short-page are not written. A request sent before B would have to be repeated anyway, so the head lands no later in practice, and the user's «catch up early» holds.
- The head keeps only trades with a time below `restart.fromMs`. A gap lies inside a held range only when its `untilMs` is below `restart.fromMs`; any other gap takes the ordinary path of mark, fetch by id and amend. That closes the straddling-gap loss.
- The task is split by the sub-numbering rule. 93.17.1.1 is the reader: a plain `TradeRangeReader` behind `ITradeRangeReader` with its own spec, `0372-trade-range-reader.md`, where `TRADE_PAGE_LIMIT`, `TRADES_SINCE_SPAN_MS` and `tradeIdOf` each get one home. 93.17.1.2 is the catch-up runs, composition, ownership, the park until B, the gap run and the straddle rule; spec `0371` is rewritten in place and names the red-suite cases that change, including those that pinned the early by-time request.
- The `ROADMAP.md` contract line of 93.17.1 became the lines of 93.17.1.1 and 93.17.1.2.

The attempt's plan, its three plan reviews and its sidecar were deleted after this report was written. The deferred observations in those reviews were not pinned: the by-time page on a long reconnect went into the reader's design, the straddling gap into the rewritten spec, and the double fold of a gap that reaches B stays with the spec that stops amending primed buckets.
