The task stopped in planning; no code was written and nothing escalated.

The task fills `PairCatchUp` under one rule: a catch-up ends at the trade before the first one the live seconds take, B. The closed minutes are asked at once. The trade fetch parks until `onTrades` shows B, then `readBefore` reads from the head minute, or from `skipped.fromMs`, to B - 1. The gap run reads through `readIds`. It also rewrites the existing suite onto this rule. The plan took the run design from the spec whole: a generation token per pair, one latest range, B scoped to that range, no timer, one error log per failed run in `SecondClock`'s call shape. All three reviews affirm that design in their positive notes. None of them raised a finding against the body of `PairCatchUp`.

The first review found two real problems, both in the rewrite of the suite, and both in the kind that passes without proving what it names. The suite's reconnect fixtures send a first batch whose reported gap ends at or after `restart.fromMs`, for example `reconnectWithGap` with `trade(5, 123_100, …)` against a start of 123_000, and the «no held close» case with `trade(4, 124_100, …)` against 124_000. Under the spec's gap rule such a gap straddles the seconds' start and takes the ordinary path: it marks the buckets and runs `readIds`. In `reconnectWithGap`, the scripted `from: [new Error('down')]` of «logs a rejected reconnect fetch once and applies nothing» would then be consumed by that gap run, while the reconnect's own `tradesSince(121_000)` stayed parked. The case would stay green, still seeing one error, `fromCalls` of length one and the same partial `5s` tuple, while proving a gap failure instead of a reconnect failure. In the «no held close» case, the gap's trades would be folded a second time into the bar of a minute or more. The review also found three smaller faults: a wrong expected result in the final `onConnected(` sweep, a `DEVIATION` recorded for conforming to the plan, and `ParsedCandle` missing from the import list. The planner reshaped the fixtures so that every first-batch gap lies below the seconds' start, and fixed the other three.

The second review, whose risk level dropped to Low, found two weaknesses in the assertions. The plan's «before B shows» checks asserted `sinceCalls` empty synchronously. The head sits behind `await run.woken`, so a head wrongly started before B would still be a microtask behind, and the assertion could not fail. The «no gap flat at the held close» case also left its counts to be derived. Both were fixed with a settle-then-assert sequence and pinned counts.

The third review, also Low, found two more places where a test could not tell the right behaviour from a near miss. The «stops paging the head at a trade at or after the first live second» case had no observable that separates the cut by id from a cut by time: the scripted page carries trades from B on whose times are still below `restart.fromMs`, so a head that cut by time alone would keep them, and the case's `fromCalls` assertion holds either way. The short-page case left B open, and a B just past the scripted pages' last id would make the read succeed instead of fail.

That is one kind of finding across all three reviews: an assertion or a fixture of the rewritten suite that can pass without proving the behaviour it names. Nothing in these findings contradicts the governing doc, and the spec's rule was followed correctly. Two things the reviews did not find matter as much. Nothing was found in the algorithm: the gap rule, the supersede-and-clear rule, the wake on a newer range, the currency check after the wake and after the settle, and the failure ordering all stood. And the severity fell and stayed low: two Medium findings in the first review, every later finding Low.

The spec traces as follows. The first review's two Mediums come from a gap the spec left open: it states the straddle rule and says the reconnect cases «keep their expectation», but never says that the landed fixtures' first-batch gaps now straddle, so a case that keeps its expectation must change its first batch to keep it. The rest is the planner's: the spec states the behaviour correctly in each case (a head not asked before B, a head ended by id, a short page a failure), and the planner missed an assertion that could fail, a count to pin, or an observable that separates id from time.

> The task spec must state that a landed case which keeps its expectation but whose first batch reports a gap ending at or after the seconds' start has its first batch reshaped so the gap lies inside the range, so that a straddling gap never silently takes an answer scripted for the head.

Classification: specification gap, small and already absorbed by the plan, with the dominant finding a plan-detail kind, test assertions that pass for the wrong reason. The recurring-issue signal is that kind. It is not scope overload: no review described the body as too much.

## What was done

The repair is at spec plus plan depth. The spec `0371` gained one sentence in its list of changed cases: a landed case that keeps its expectation but whose first batch reports a gap ending at or after `restart.fromMs` (`reconnectWithGap` and the «no held close» case among them) gets its first batch reshaped so the gap lies inside the range, the closing trade delivered below the seconds' start and B following it.

The plan was kept and every review's fix folded in. The first and second reviews' fixes were already in it, as the later reviews confirmed (the reshaped `reconnectWithGap` with the rejected case asserting the reconnect's own failure, the «no held close» first batch, the corrected `onConnected(` sweep hits, the `DEVIATION` only on the `batch` route, the `ParsedCandle` import, the settle-then-assert «before B» checks, the pinned counts of «no gap flat at the held close»). The third review's fixes were added: the «stops paging the head at a trade at or after the first live second» case asserts the `1m` bucket's volume holds the trades up to `B - 1` only, stating that a time cut would keep the page's trades from B on and show a larger volume, with no derived number; the short-page case connects with a B of 2500, above the scripted pages' last id, asserts one error and no `prime`, and names the `1h` bar that goes out partial holding only the live trade. No other pin was added.

The three plan reviews were deleted. The sidecar was rolled back to `planned:1` with the `implementer` and `escalation` keys absent and every other key kept; the plan and the sidecar stay.

The split into «reshape the red suite» and «fill the body» was considered and rejected. A review of a task holding only red tests judges them by imagining wrong implementations, with no floor: no assertion ever runs, and each round names a new one. With the body in the same task, the reviewer checks each assertion against the implementation. Every case also drives a connect through the same harness, so a split would leave the gap cases red until the head is built, and the reader, the one seam that split cleanly, has already landed.
