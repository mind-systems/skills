# 55.7, fourth time — three numbers that recruited their own work

**Project:** tradeoxy_core
**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Stopped at:** planned:3
**Elapsed before the rescue:** 8645

The fourth rescue of this task, and the first where the severity of what the readings found rose rather than fell. Twelve readings now across four runs. The rise is real and its cause is not the task.

Four findings. The first is the most serious anything in this lineage has produced. A route parameter's pipe, bound by class reference rather than by instance, can fail to resolve without anything saying so: the unresolved class becomes a null that the pipes context quietly filters out of its own array, the consumer then folds over what remains, and with nothing remaining the raw value passes through untouched. Every one of the twenty-one doors would accept any string. Nothing reaches it — the compiler sees no dependency graph, and not one suite in the repository boots any of the five controllers that carry such a pipe; the only controller any testing module registers anywhere is a remote-call one with no route parameters at all. The plan had diagnosed this failure mode precisely, in its own words, and then written no check for it. The second finding, an import edit split across two tasks where one is droppable, the plan had already closed by the time the run ended.

The third and fourth are the rescuer's own. A vendor-reference document written earlier the same day — on this coordinator's order, at the user's request to preserve a line of reasoning somewhere durable — closed with three measured numbers: how many request-shape files the codebase carries, how many validator decorators sit across them, and how many further sites two project-authored wrappers occupy. The task falsifies two of the three on contact, since it removes four decorator applications and adds a third wrapper. Worse, the headline number was wrong when it was written: the tree measures seventy-eight by the document's own stated rule, or seventy-five under a stricter reading, against the eighty recorded. And by the time a reader reached it, the plan had grown four separate pieces of machinery whose only purpose was to keep that number in step — a context item, a settings change, a task, and an acceptance-run bullet.

The rule that forbids this is the user's own and was already written in the pair's working memory when the coordinator broke it: a count of a growable set does not belong in a durable artifact, because a number goes only where something will notice when it drifts. In a document nothing notices. What this run demonstrated is the part the rule does not say out loud — such a number does not merely rot quietly. It recruits work. The next honest reader is obliged to check it, and the machinery to maintain it accretes around it in every artifact that touches the same ground.

One correction belongs here, because the coordinator's first account of this was wrong. The document was not introduced between the third run and this one. It existed throughout the third run, whose own first reading opened it and praised it — having checked its prose against the design and never its numbers against the diff. So it did not raise severity by appearing; the gate simply reached one layer deeper than it had before, onto ground a reader had already passed over once.

> A count of a growable set, written into a durable document, was wrong on the day it was written, falsified further by the change under review, and had already grown four pieces of maintenance machinery across the plan before any reader reached it.

**Root-cause category:** not a defect of the task — two of four findings are the rescuer's own artifact, and one of the remaining two the plan had already closed. **Severity, measured across twelve readings:** three findings in the whole lineage could have shipped wrong behaviour — a request body rendering as an object in the client-facing schema, a guard removed before the value it guards stopped being asserted, and now a pipe binding nothing proves. The first two are closed and have not returned. The remaining eighteen were instruction precision, comments, imports, and these three numbers.

## What was done

The numbers came out of the document rather than being corrected, because correcting them would have left the defect in place and kept the maintenance machinery alive. What the passage argues survives without them: a switch to schema-first validation replaces the global validation pipe and every validator decorator site across the request-shape layer, together with the project's own wrappers, named rather than counted, so the list describes itself and commits to no number that can drift. The section's stance — neither recommending the switch nor arguing against it — is untouched, as is the rest of the file, which a reader had already checked and endorsed. With the numbers gone, all four pieces of machinery came out of the plan in one stroke, and both of that run's later findings ceased to have a subject.

The specification gained one clause, for the binding: that what must be proven is not only the constructor's own behaviour but that it resolves through the injector at a real door — stated as the obligation, with the construction left to the plan. The plan then gained that construction, pinned against the repository rather than assumed: a real container booted the way the repository's own precedent boots one, a single real controller with a stubbed collaborator, a listening application, and two requests — a malformed identifier answered by a refusal, and a well-formed one in the wrong case answered with the value canonicalised.

One thing in that pinning is worth recording, because it is the second time in a day the same discipline paid. The coordinator's work-order described the discriminating mechanism from a reader's account rather than from the code: that an empty collaborator stub would make an unbound pipe answer with a server error rather than a refusal. The hand checked it against the controller and found the claim could not hold — an empty stub has no method to call at all, so every request that reaches the handler fails identically, and the second assertion the same construction has to carry would have been impossible. It substituted a stub that implements the one method and re-derived what each assertion discriminates on separately: the malformed request on its status, the well-formed one on the value that comes back. Earlier the same day the same discipline ran the other way, when the hand's report claimed two findings were already closed in the plan and they were open. Neither party's account survives contact with the files; that is what the measuring is for.

Three readings were deleted; the plan and its planning session were kept, and the run resumes at the reading of the plan.
