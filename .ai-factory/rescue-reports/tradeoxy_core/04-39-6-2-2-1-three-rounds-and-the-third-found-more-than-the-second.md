# 39.6.2.2.1 — three rounds of review, and the third found more than the second

**Project:** tradeoxy_core
**Date:** 2026-09-09
**Stopped at:** planned:3
**Elapsed before the rescue:** 4155

The task asks for little: fill the constructor and `drive` of a registry that already ships as a skeleton, swap one injection, and hold a per-run registry of engines for the length of a run. The plan for it was written a week earlier, orphaned by a tree-wide commit that made its artifacts read as a completed task, and never reviewed. It was resurrected deliberately, to see where it would stumble, and the first review round put it at high risk on four counts — one of which did not belong to the plan at all.

The first round stumbled on a claim the plan had inherited from its requirement rather than invented: that neither hand-assembled test module carries `OrdersGrpcClient`, and so both should receive it as a stub. For the enrichment suite that is true. For the integration suite it is not — the token is already imported, declared, assigned, registered as a provider and scripted by two scenarios. A second binding would shadow the first, and the ownership probe that shipped with the preceding task would then call `fetchOrders` on an object without it: a synchronous type error inside a working check, and nearly every test in the file falls with it. Alongside that, the plan carried three of the requirement's four seam additions — the ownership probe was absent entirely, together with its ordering constraint and the scenario the requirement mandates for it; the sixth unit scenario that discriminates the replay-mode flag on the event bus was missing, with the plan resting instead on five shipped scenarios that cannot show it; and the module file whose ten-line justification comment goes stale was never named.

The second round came back at low risk, and all four of its findings were new — created by the first round's repairs. The scenario written to discriminate the bus flag was pinned to the millisecond on its timestamps and left unpinned on the one value its discrimination turns on, the alert's kind: scripted as a trigger it returns nothing against a correct constructor and a broken one alike, so it fails for a reason unrelated to what it tests. Deleting four scenarios changed module-scoped mock state that a surviving test inherits, while the plan said that test is left exactly as it is. And two citations were imprecise — a gateway named without its directory, and a count of call sites that was one short.

The third round returned at medium risk, which is not a regression but a deepening: the first two rounds examined artifacts, and the third reached behaviour. The probe, placed first and ahead of the registry membership, is a real network round trip inside a block whose cancellation check sits above it. A cancel arriving while the probe is in flight finds the run's subscription id still unset, so the eviction and the release both run against nothing; the probe then resolves and execution proceeds to take the membership and the stream reference that nothing will now free. Both leak. The asymmetry matters and the plan does not state it: the acquire's own await is already safe, because by then the id is assigned and teardown reaches both. No existing scenario covers the window.

Three further findings came with it — two more clauses of the module comment that this change narrows, a stub with no stated home in the enrichment suite where both reachable homes are wrong, and eight new scenarios placed after a test that reads an accumulator everything declared before it feeds.

> The requirement pinned the harness's provider set from a measurement taken before the preceding task shipped, and pinned the probe's position without pinning the consequence of its being awaited — that its own await re-opens the cancellation window the check above it exists to close.

**Root-cause category:** specification gap. **Recurring:** at the class level rather than the instance — every blocking finding of all three rounds sat in the harness, the module comment, or the seam's ordering, and none touched the production design, which was re-verified line by line in each round and confirmed three times.

## What was done

The repair went past any single depth, and that is worth stating rather than smoothing over: what the diagnosis proposed was a spec-and-plan repair, and what landed was a spec rewrite, a decomposition, a reordering, and the reassignment of one artifact to a different task.

**The task was split**, against its own requirement's argument. That requirement rejected a split on the ground that a registry-only half is never fed and so is not independently revertable behaviour. The project's own gate does not ask for behaviour — it asks whether the half compiles, breaks nothing, and delivers independently observable value — and the registry half does: it turns five shipped red scenarios green. The phase had already shipped a task by that same standard, a skeleton with red scenarios, which the behaviour test would have forbidden. The empirical argument settled it: three rounds found nothing in the constructor and drive bodies, and every blocking finding lay in the wiring, the module comment, and the test suite. The seam was drawn where the failures had already drawn it. The atomicity paragraph was corrected rather than deleted, so that it now names the boundary that does hold.

**The retirement task was moved up**, to sit immediately after the wiring rather than after the coverage task. The wiring turns a run-scoped exemption in the runtime into one that silently freezes a live activation whose configuration names a subscription an active run is using; the retirement closes it. Leaving the order as it was would have held that window open across two tasks. Moving it costs nothing: the coverage task depends on the wiring, not on the retirement, and the retirement's own dependency is on the engines having moved, which the wiring is what does.

**The module comment was reassigned in full.** The comment keeps an inventory — for each import, which of its exports is injected and by whom — and this change falsifies three of its clauses. Three review rounds each caught one, because the plan repaired the clause it was shown. One clause is deleted outright by a later task in the same phase; the other two were owned by nobody. The whole block therefore goes to that later task, which already opens the file, and it loses the inventory while keeping what only it can say: the reasoning about acyclicity and where a forward reference is and is not needed. The wiring task is now barred from touching that file at all, by a guard placed against the movement the previous attempt actually made.

**Positional addressing was removed** from the requirement, from the later task's own requirement, and from its contract line. A reference now addresses by name. The requirement shrank by roughly a third without losing a single mandated scenario.

**Two findings were promoted out of the review and into the requirement**, so that they stop being rediscovered each round: the cancellation re-check after the probe, together with why the acquire needs none, and the correction that the enrichment suite's double is inline rather than named.

**The transient state was cleared, and the depth it ended at is not the one the diagnosis proposed.** The three review files were removed, and so were the plan and its sidecar; the tracked pair's removal is staged, so they leave the repository when the change is committed. Nothing for that slug remains on disk. In the vocabulary of the depths, that is the full reset — plan, reviews and sidecar all gone — rather than the plan-preserving rollback the diagnosis argued for, and the split is why: the original task's title changed when it became the second of two children, so its slug changed with it. The plan on disk belonged to a task that no longer exists under that name; scanning by slug would never have adopted it, and rolling its resume point back to a first attempt would have preserved a resume point into nothing. Both new tasks now plan from scratch, which is what the rewritten requirement is written for.

Sidecar deleted (full reset).
