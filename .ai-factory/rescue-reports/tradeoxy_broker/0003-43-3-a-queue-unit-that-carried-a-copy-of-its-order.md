# 43.3 — a queue unit that carried a copy of its order

**Project:** tradeoxy_broker
**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Stopped at:** escalated
**Elapsed before the rescue:** absent

No code was written for this task. The planner stopped because it could not plan honestly without a decision outside its authority.

The spec told the router to resolve the affected subscriptions, run the planning step, and hand one unit per planned item to that subscription's queue, where the unit's run would do nothing but call the apply step on its item. The planner examined what the item holds. The work item, landed two tasks earlier, carries the open order exactly as the planning step read it, together with a close percent computed against that order's remainder at the moment of reading. That reading happens in the router, outside the subscription's queue. The apply step then appends its slice to that copy and writes the whole order back, including the entire list of executed takes. The planner found this at odds with the concurrency doc's statement that the subscription's queue is the only place its orders are mutated. It laid out four options: accept the spec as written and record a follow-up; re-read the order inside the queue but keep the planned percent; carry the attributed quantity and derive the percent in the queue from the current remainder; or move all planning into the queue, which leaves no single moment to split one exchange fill across several subscriptions.

The planner's case rested on a race: an exchange close arriving while a strategy of the same subscription is mid-close. The user rejected that framing as an impossible scenario. If our own partial close completes, the exchange executed it before the liquidation; if it does not, its wait ends in an error and the queue moves on. The actual rule is simpler and was already practiced by every strategy: a unit is created holding only the subscription and the parameters its command needs, and during its own run it loads fresh data, processes it, and writes it back; only then is its work done. `SubscriptionExitStrategy` already works this way, holding a subscription, a direction and a percent, and loading its order inside its run. The governing docs already put the decision on disk. `docs/watcher/order-routing.md` § Liquidation and ADL says the exchange-initiated sync path, once inside the queue, "finds the order by symbol and direction, computes the close percent, and appends a fact slice". The phase note let each unit "carry its own share of the filled quantity". The task specs of the phase carried neither: the work-item task gave the item a copy of the order and a percent frozen on a remainder read outside the queue, and this task inherited that shape. So this was not a new decision. It was the third of the planner's options, already made by the governing doc and never passed down.

> The work item should have carried only the subscription, the close direction, its attributed share of the filled quantity and the average price, with the apply step loading the open order and computing the close percent from the current remainder during its own run, as `order-routing.md` already states.

## What was done

Escalation option 1, decision recorded, then a full reset. The work-item shape is a separate reason to revert from the router change, and the tasks that landed it are closed. So the decision became a new task, `43.2.3`, with spec `0049`, placed before `43.3`. In it the item carries the subscription, the close direction, the attributed quantity (clamped to the candidate's own remainder) and the average price, and nothing else. The apply step loads the open order via `getOpenOrder(subscriptionId:direction:)` during its own run and computes the remaining quantity through one computation it shares with the planning step. It derives the close percent from that fresh remainder, clamped at 100, and logs and returns without writing when no open order or no remainder is left. `handleOrderFill` keeps its plan-then-apply shape unchanged. The distribute tests move from asserting percents to asserting attributed quantities. The two DB-backed sync-manager test classes stay as the behavior guard.

The rule itself went into the governing doc, `docs/strategy/strategy-dispatcher-concurrency.md` § The subscription isolation principle: a unit holds only the subscription and its command's parameters, loads what it acts on fresh during its run, and is finished only once its write lands, exchange units included. Spec `0043` and its contract line gained the dependency on `43.2.3`, and `0043`'s description of the unit's stored item now points to it. Spec `0046` (`43.4`) was checked and survives unchanged. The specs of the closed tasks, `0042` and `0044`, were left as history. The plan and sidecar for `43.3` were deleted; there were no review files. The orchestrator re-plans `43.3` after `43.2.3` lands.
