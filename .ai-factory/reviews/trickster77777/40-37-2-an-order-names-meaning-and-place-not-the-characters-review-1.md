### Findings

1. **Dangling pronoun — "It does not compose the sentence" has no antecedent.** (`src/skills/agent-architect/SKILL.md`, § "Relay on the marker…", the sentence following "name what a sentence must say and where it goes — the meaning and the place.") The plan's requirement 1 and the governing spec both phrase the rule with a subject: "An order names what a sentence must say and where it goes. It does not compose the sentence." The implementation turned the first clause into an imperative addressed to *you* ("name what a sentence must say…") and kept the second clause's "It" — so "It" now has nothing to point at; the nearest nouns are "channel-message" and "place." In executable prose an instruction whose subject the reader has to guess is the same defect 37.1 just repaired in the sentence immediately above (its opening pronoun). Fix: give the clause its subject — e.g. "…channel-message: the order names what a sentence must say and where it goes — the meaning and the place — and does not compose the sentence: the text is written by the editor, who has the file open." Any form that makes *the order* the explicit subject of "does not compose" resolves it.

2. **Change-history phrasing inside the shipped rule — "stay pinned in the order exactly as before."** (same paragraph) "As before" names a prior version of this file that a reader on a fresh invocation has never seen; it is the spec's description of *the change* ("stay pinned exactly as before") copied into the rule itself. The global authoring rule is present tense, no change narration, and the sentence loses nothing without it: "…these are the thing itself, not a description of it, and stay pinned in the order." Same, more mildly, for "An anchor is **still** addressed by name" — "still" reads as continuity-within-the-rule and can stay, but if you touch the sentence, "An anchor is addressed by name — … — never a position" is the present-tense form.

Neither finding changes what the rule means; both are wording defects in the instruction a reader executes, and the first leaves the central sentence of the task without a subject. No security or runtime concerns — the change is prose in a skill body with no machine-scanned token touched.
